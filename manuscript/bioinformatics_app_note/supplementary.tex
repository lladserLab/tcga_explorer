\section*{Scope and Reproduction}

This supplement documents a retrospective software evaluation. It does not
constitute prognostic-marker validation, causal inference or a prospective
sample-size design. Endpoint availability requires at least 10 linked
participants and five events; adjusted models are screened again for five
events and 10 complete participants after covariate filtering. The exported audit reports
retain participant selection, exclusions, group and event counts, covariate
completeness and model diagnostics in accordance with the reporting intent of
REMARK \citep{remark2012}. Audit schema v4 stores separate patient records and
SHA-256 hashes for the full continuous population and the cutpoint-specific
grouped population and binds the complete report to a detached server receipt.
Every Cox model also records its fitted parameter count and events per
parameter. Values below 10 are flagged as low-information and values below 5
as severe. Low-information, unstable, non-finite or extreme standard fits add
a \texttt{coxphf} Firth sensitivity with penalty 0.5, profile
penalized-likelihood inference and Breslow ties; this remains separate from the
standard Efron-ties Cox estimate \citep{heinzeSchemper2001}.

Unless a user filter restricts sample types, selection prioritizes primary
tumor or primary blood-derived cancer, recurrent tumor, additional new primary,
metastatic tumor, tumor-derived cells, normal tissue and control samples, in
that order, after samples without the requested gene or complete signature score
have been removed; RNA analyte/portion metadata and barcode resolve ties. The
audit records participants for whom a lower-priority expression-complete sample
replaced a higher-priority candidate lacking the requested score. Stage 0--IV
and grade 1--5 are encoded as numeric ordinal trends after substage collapse.
An exact user-selected complete-case model can include age per 10 years,
ordinal stage or grade, and categorical GDC gender or race. Unknown and
nonstandard categories become missing. Fixed stage/grade fits remain
auxiliary; an unavailable requested model is not replaced by another
adjustment.

The default browser path is one gene, OS, log2(TPM+1), no clinical filters,
age adjustment and a median grouped sensitivity. Continuous Cox is the primary
display; the spline is added only when its information threshold is met.
Kaplan--Meier, grouped Cox and RMST remain cutpoint sensitivities. Audit,
reconstruction and signed-receipt downloads are generated for every completed
run. Prespecified multiverse execution is a separate explicit action.
The backend publishes an OpenAPI 3 schema and interactive Swagger/ReDoc
documentation; the dependency-free \path{scripts/tcga_trace_cli.py} client
submits, polls, downloads and verifies the same asynchronous analysis
families.

The frozen benchmarks are generated through the public HTTP interface with:

\begin{verbatim}
scripts/publication/run_single_gene_benchmark_suite.py
scripts/publication/run_feature_benchmarks.py
scripts/publication/run_reproducibility_benchmark.py
scripts/publication/run_server_attestation_benchmark.py --check-only
scripts/publication/run_clean_reproduction_benchmark.py --build --platform native
scripts/publication/run_runtime_benchmark.py
scripts/publication/capture_reviewer_screenshots.py
\end{verbatim}

The marker panel was assembled after exploratory triage on 23 July 2026 and is
therefore not presented as prospectively selected biomarker validation. Before
subsequent regeneration, the exact 11 scenarios, literature anchors, endpoint
roles and change-control rule were frozen in
\path{docs/publication/benchmark/scenario_registry_v1.json}. OS is the default
primary endpoint; UVM BAP1 DSS is the registered disease-specific exception.
BRCA MKI67 PFI and LUAD CD274 PFI are endpoint sensitivities linked to OS
parents and cannot replace those parents because of a more favourable result.
The registry's exact SHA-256 is copied into every scenario metadata file.

For every two-group run, the RMST horizon is defined without group labels as
the smaller of five years and the 75th percentile of observed endpoint times in
the unstratified, expression-complete eligible cohort. Both groups must support
the horizon and retain at least five participants at risk. Sensitivity
estimates use 0.75, 0.90 and 1.00 times that fixed horizon; reported differences
are comparison minus reference (high minus low for standard two-group runs)
\citep{rmstBiomarker2016}.

\section*{Competing-Risk Estimands}

TCGA-CDR \texttt{ExtraEndpoints} columns encode DSS, DFI and PFI as
\texttt{0=censored}, \texttt{1=event of interest} and
\texttt{2=endpoint-specific competing death}. For DSS these are index-cancer
death and other-cause death; for DFI they are documented recurrence and death
before recurrence; for PFI they are progression/death with tumour and death
without preceding progression. Kaplan--Meier and Cox outputs censor code 2 at
its recorded time and therefore retain an event-free/cause-specific
interpretation. Companion cumulative-incidence functions retain code 2,
compare groups with Gray's test \citep{gray1988}, and report pointwise
Aalen-variance intervals and numbers at risk at one, three and five years.
Values with fewer than five at risk remain visible with a support caution.

Grouped and continuous Fine--Gray models estimate subdistribution HRs for code
1 while retaining code 2 in the risk-set construction
\citep{fineGray1999}. They use the requested exact complete-case adjustment
and the same available auxiliary stage/grade specifications as Cox, and report
model-specific participants, endpoint events, competing events, fitted
parameters, events per parameter, coding and contrasts. Cause-specific HRs and
subdistribution HRs are not interchangeable. The exact source columns,
patient-level status, CIF/Gray/Fine--Gray output, plots, package version and R
helper are retained in methodology, audit and standalone reproduction
artifacts.

head-to-head performance benchmark, and it does not use basic survival

``Residual comparison point'' means that the cited source did not establish the
complete TCGA-TRACE reporting contract as one output; it does not prove that a
capability is absent from every current version. The resources are

The compact table emphasizes resources closest to the submitted workflow.
Additional source classes materially constrain the novelty claim. PrognoScan
curated multi-cohort microarray survival and corrected its within-search
minimum-\(p\) cutpoint, while explicitly warning about cohort, platform and
random-error heterogeneity \citep{prognoscan2009}. OncoLnc linked TCGA mRNA,
miRNA and lncRNA expression to downloadable survival data
\citep{oncolnc2016}. ESurv added continuous analysis, user data and penalized
multi-omic signature construction beside median and optimized grouping
\citep{esurv2020}. These are established precedents for gene screening,
cross-dataset interrogation, optimized grouping and downloadable coupled data.

Gene-set and broader transcriptomic systems also exceed TCGA-TRACE in important
dimensions. GSCA applies GSVA to gene sets across genomic, immunogenomic and
pharmacogenomic modules \citep{gsca2023}; TCGEx combines survival modelling
with enrichment, clustering, machine learning and user datasets
\citep{tcgex2025}; and Survival Genie 2 spans 132 adult and paediatric datasets
and connects single-cell-derived markers, modules and ligand--receptor pairs to
bulk survival cohorts \citep{survivalGenie2026}. CaPSSA provides interactive
patient stratification by mutation, copy number and expression
\citep{capssa2019}. SurvBoard addresses a different target---standardized
benchmarking of multi-omic survival prediction, including calibration and
cross-program evaluation---rather than interactive marker-association records
\citep{survboard2025}.

The recurring boundaries are therefore methodological scope, not generic
software defects. Outcome-optimized grouping still requires explicit
selection and multiplicity interpretation; broad portals prioritize assay,
dataset or pathway coverage; and programmable workflows can be fully
reproducible but leave endpoint harmonization, sample selection and reporting
structure to the analyst. TCGA-TRACE does not resolve biological confounding,
external validation or predictive-model benchmarking. It tests whether one
TCGA marker-analysis run can preserve its cohort construction, estimands,
diagnostics, specification family and executable reconstruction together.

The comparison therefore does not support a claim that TCGA-TRACE is broader or
statistically more advanced than these resources. PESSA has stronger
pathway-scoring and dataset breadth, KM Plotter has broader assay screening,
cSurvival has purpose-built two-predictor cutoff algorithms, Survival Genie 2
has broader dataset and single-cell-derived input support, and the R packages
offer greater programmable flexibility. The tested contribution is the
integration of endpoint and sample provenance, specification-level
multiplicity, diagnostics, executable reconstruction and a server-origin
receipt. No independent feature audit of every comparator version was
performed.

11 single-gene cases are \path{LIHC/CDC20/OS},

\path{LUAD/CD274/OS} and \path{LUAD/CD274/PFI}. The six broader workflows
are \path{ACC/BUB1B-PINK1/OS}, \path{UVM/BAP1xPRAME/DSS},
\path{PAN-CANCER/BIRC5/OS}, \path{KIRC/HYPOXIA/OS},
\path{SKCM/EFFECTORxEXHAUSTION/OS} and
\path{PAN-CANCER/CA9/OS}. Thus, Tables S4--S6 cover all 17 web examples,
including null and discordant outputs; the complete per-method records remain
in the accompanying CSV and JSON files.

The four biological cases in the main text were selected for exposition after
the frozen outputs were available: two show within-TCGA literature concordance,
one provides cross-cohort/cross-assay directional corroboration, and one is
deliberately non-confirmatory. Only the ACC case uses a cohort and assay
independent of the TCGA-TRACE analysis; none is prospective clinical
validation. This presentation set does not define a multiplicity family and
does not replace the complete registered panel.

All examples use log2(TPM + 1). Mean and weighted signatures are complete-case
averages of raw transformed expression. Z-score signatures standardize each
gene among the endpoint-complete, expression-complete participants in the run
and use \(\sum_g w_g z_{ig}/\sum_g |w_g|\). These compact scores test
workflow behaviour and are not substitutes for pathway-activity methods such as
ssGSEA. The ACC contrast is an RNA-seq analogue of a published qRT-PCR score,
not a replication of its assay or threshold \citep{fragoso2012bub1b}. The UVM
gene-expression groups likewise do not replace BAP1 immunohistochemistry or a clinical PRAME
classifier \citep{han2023bap1prame}.

Pan-cancer cohort rows retain the Cox HR per within-cohort score SD for
descriptive comparison. These effects are not pooled. For single-gene, mean
and weighted scores, the standardized coefficient and standard error are
divided by that cohort's score SD to recover the algebraically equivalent
effect per one shared input-score unit. Only effects with the same endpoint and
adjustment family enter REML synthesis with HKSJ inference; a 95\% prediction
interval is reported for at least three cohorts. Z-score signatures are
standardized inside each cohort and therefore have no transportable raw score
unit; their pooled estimate is intentionally unavailable.

\section*{Executable Audit Benchmark}

The verifier recomputes participant, scoring, result and artifact hashes;
reconstructs single-gene, weighted or crossed-signature scores from audited
components; and re-executes the frozen R input. Each two-group export additionally carries
that input, the exact R engine and helpers, a standalone runner,
\texttt{renv.lock}, a Dockerfile fixed to an immutable
\texttt{rocker/r-ver} digest and instructions. Three representative capsules
were run with a read-only root and capsule, disabled network and no application
source, TCGA matrix or database; only temporary and output directories were
writable. Native arm64 and locally emulated amd64 produced 6/6 core-result
matches under the recorded quantity-specific
\(\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\times\max(|x|,|y|)\) policy, with exact matching
for counts and categorical fields. An independent GitHub-hosted Linux/amd64 job
repeats this contract. Internal check counts describe coverage within three
frozen analyses and are not independent replications. Request, participant,
scoring and source-data mutations changed the reproducibility hash; plot and
methods mutations remained
outside that hash but failed artifact checksums. The threat model is accidental
drift, corruption or incomplete transfer for the internal hashes alone.

Audit schema v4 adds a detached Ed25519 receipt that signs the exact audit byte
count, SHA-256, schema and recorded reproducibility hash. The private key is
stored in a persistent server volume outside the application images; the
corresponding key ID and raw public key are available through the HTTPS API.
The frozen BAP1/UVM DSS receipt verified against its archived public key. Two
negative controls were then applied: changing a generation-time field caused
the exact-report binding to fail, and recomputing both the altered report
SHA-256 and canonical payload SHA-256 still failed Ed25519 verification because
the original signature could not be recomputed. This extends the threat model
to an exporter without the server private key, conditional on trusting the
HTTPS or independently archived key fingerprint. It does not establish
scientific correctness, prespecification, append-only time, or protection
against server-key compromise.
The benchmark key's full SHA-256 fingerprint is
\path{5f6765ef7ec0845078c4344f2542c945492bea3f38427d6ab909def3e9c01643}.
The HTTPS endpoint is a discovery mechanism; the archived release fingerprint
is the long-term trust anchor. Routine rotations retain prior key IDs as
\texttt{retired}; a compromised key is published as \texttt{revoked}, and the
verifier rejects its trust status even if a historical signature remains
cryptographically valid. This policy and its limits are versioned with the
release.

\section*{Prespecified Multiverse Contract}

Pipeline \texttt{prespecified-multiverse-family-v1.1} freezes a Cartesian
endpoint-by-scoring-by-cutpoint grid before execution, with a maximum of 72
specifications. Its primary continuous family contains each unique endpoint
and scoring combination once, even when several cutpoints reuse the same
expression-complete population. Its grouped sensitivity family contains every
declared endpoint, scoring and cutpoint combination. BH and Bonferroni
corrections are computed independently within the two families. For maxstat,
the Lau94 selection-adjusted rank-test p-value enters grouped multiplicity;
the grouped HR, confidence interval and RMST remain explicitly
post-selection.

The parent ledger stores every planned specification, including failures and
endpoint-QC exclusions, with the normalized request hash, child analysis ID,
child audit hash and status. Its family hash binds the declared dimensions,
data and pipeline versions, and ledger. The export contains result JSON,
continuous and grouped CSV tables, an SVG specification curve, ledger JSON,
audit JSON/HTML, parameter-specific methodology and a ZIP bundle. It computes
no retained/not-retained verdict, and marker-term and global PH diagnostics
remain interpretation fields. The ledger is scoped to the declared
multiverse; it does not imply that unrelated ad hoc browser runs were
prespecified.

The browser integration test declares TCGA-LIHC CDC20 OS with single-gene
scoring and median plus upper-quartile cutpoints. It requires 2/2 planned
grouped cells, one deduplicated continuous reference, visible family-specific
q-values, a rendered curve, the complete ledger and no horizontal page
overflow at 390 pixels. This is a software-contract test, not biomarker
validation.

\section*{Exploratory Session-History Contract}

The optional browser history is disabled by default and remains local until
export. It stores server job identifiers, timestamps, status and compact design
labels, but not patient records or uploaded covariate-row values. A user may
export up to 200 selected events. The backend resolves the exact requests and
results from those job identifiers, retains repeated execution events and
deduplicates exact hypotheses.

Continuous Cox, grouped cutpoint and two-signature interaction tests form
separate post hoc BH and Bonferroni families. Multiverse and pan-cancer jobs
retain their internal correction and are referenced without entering these
families again. Failed, expired, missing or non-terminal sources remain in the
ledger with their status and, when excluded, a reason. JSON, run and hypothesis
CSVs, methodology,
ledger, audit HTML/JSON and a signed receipt state that the report covers only
the selected export and cannot establish that no other analyses occurred.

\section*{Continuous and Cutpoint Diagnostics}

Each of the 11 scenarios first fits one cutpoint-independent continuous Cox
model over all eligible expression-complete participants. Expression is
standardized within the analysis, so the primary effect is an HR per one score
standard deviation. A three-degree-of-freedom restricted cubic spline uses
knots at the 5th, 35th, 65th and 95th expression percentiles; a
likelihood-ratio test compares it with the linear model and the effect profile
uses median expression as reference \citep{harrell2015rms}. The spline's three
fitted parameters require at least 30 events, corresponding to 10 events per
parameter; UVM BAP1 (21 events) is therefore explicitly not evaluable. BH
correction is applied separately to the 11 primary linear tests and the 10
evaluable nonlinearity tests. The registered catalogue contains six
BH-supported linear effects and one BH-supported nonlinear effect (KIRC CA9).
These are descriptions of a panel assembled after exploratory triage, not
prospective rates or a combined decision rule.

The same scenarios are crossed with four nonredundant two-group rules:
maxstat, median, upper quartile and outer quartiles. The custom
percentile and three-group tertile options remain available in the application
but were excluded from this frozen two-group panel to avoid a redundant
publication comparison \citep{altmanRoyston2006,cutpoints2025}. Holm adjustment
is applied within each
marker--endpoint scenario to these four dependent sensitivities. Maxstat
contributes its Lau94 selection-adjusted rank-test p-value; the other rules
contribute their log-rank p-values. Lau94 values are bounded to \([0,1]\), with
the raw approximation and a clamping flag retained; two null-like scenarios
required this bound. Each row reports the within-scenario Holm p-value,
univariable and prespecified age-adjusted Cox estimates, fixed-horizon RMST,
marker-term PH, global PH and, when
applicable, the selection-adjusted maxstat rank-test p-value. The dependent
four-test Holm family was conservative under null calibration; failure to
support a grouped family is not evidence that a continuous effect is absent.
No composite
retention rule is applied because log-rank, Cox and RMST are correlated
summaries of the same outcomes, whereas PH evaluates a model assumption. A
marker-term PH caution modifies interpretation of the HR but does not exclude
the analysis; a global-only caution may arise from another covariate. For each
model with marker-term \texttt{cox.zph} \(p<0.05\)
\citep{grambschTherneau1994}, a prespecified diagnostic splits follow-up at two
years (730.5 days) and estimates marker HRs for 0--2 years and \(>2\) years
plus their ratio. Fixed one- and five-year splits repeat the same diagnostic as
sensitivities. No split is selected from expression, event times or effects.
The counting-process Cox fit uses Efron ties and participant-clustered robust
variance; the late-to-early ratio and confidence interval are Wald contrasts
of the marker-by-period interaction. Each split requires at least five events
per period and 10 participants entering the late period; otherwise its status
and support failure are exported. The two-period model is a coarse
approximation to a potentially smooth effect and is not a primary test,
replacement estimand or retention criterion.
Downstream p-values are not presented as a separate multiplicity-controlled
confirmatory family. Maxstat-grouped HRs, confidence intervals and RMST
comparisons remain post-selection. The selected adjusted estimate is the exact
age model; no stage/grade fallback is substituted when it is not evaluable.
Fixed stage, grade and stage-plus-grade models remain in the raw output as
auxiliary sensitivities.
Parameter counts, events per parameter and any triggered Firth estimate are
retained in the machine-readable per-method records. UVM BAP1 triggered three
grouped Firth sensitivities after non-finite standard intervals, and LGG EMP3
maxstat triggered one because the standard marker estimate exceeded the
prespecified extreme-effect threshold.

To compare maxstat with a cutpoint-independent reference on a common scale,
the continuous log-HR per SD was multiplied by the observed difference in
group-mean expression measured in SD units for each split. The resulting
continuous-implied group HR was compared with the grouped HR by their absolute
log-HR ratio. This is a descriptive amplification diagnostic and does not
correct the grouped estimate or interval for selection. UVM BAP1 maxstat was
not included because the selected high group had zero events and its standard
grouped Cox estimate was non-finite. Across the 10 finite scenarios, the median
of the maxstat-to-median absolute log-HR ratios was 1.80; the median maxstat
amplification relative to the continuous-implied group contrast was 1.88.
These are two distinct summary statistics. The complete diagnostic remains
available as a machine-readable CSV; its compact scenario-level amplification
column is reported in Supplementary Table S4.

CA9/KIRC illustrates how the nonlinear deliverable should be read. Its
nonlinearity test had BH \(q=0.022\). Relative to median expression
(8.630 log2(TPM+1)), profile HRs were 1.89 (1.17--3.06) at the 5th percentile,
1.42 (1.18--1.71) at the 25th, 0.86 (0.77--0.96) at the 75th and 1.02
(0.62--1.69) at the 95th. Thus the omnibus result is accompanied by a
median-referenced shape showing excess risk concentrated at low expression;
it is not converted into an optimized prognostic threshold.

For the registered UVM BAP1 DSS median split, TCGA-CDR encoded 21
disease-specific deaths and two other-cause deaths among 80 participants.
Gray's test gave \(p=0.00046\), and the unadjusted Fine--Gray high-versus-low
subdistribution HR was 0.17 (0.06--0.52; \(p=0.0016\)). The continuous
Fine--Gray HR per expression SD was 0.50 (0.38--0.68). With only two competing
deaths, these estimates are a sensitivity to the cause-specific Cox result,
not evidence that competing events are fully characterized.

The registered literature anchors cover CDC20/LIHC, BIRC5/LUAD, BAP1/UVM,
EMP3/LGG, CA9/KIRC, MKI67/BRCA, PDCD1/SKCM, TMEM176B/SKCM and CD274/LUAD

replications. The
complete four-row estimates for every scenario, including thresholds,
confidence intervals, fixed-\(\tau\) RMST sensitivity and hashes, are provided
as machine-readable benchmark files. In TCGA-SKCM, separate all-eligible,
primary-only and metastatic-only runs for PDCD1 and TMEM176B used separate
three-test BH families for continuous Cox and grouped log-rank results. For
both markers, the all-eligible and metastatic-only effects had \(q<0.05\) in
both families, whereas primary-only effects did not. This demonstrates why
sample composition is preserved as an explicit sensitivity setting rather than
an implicit preprocessing choice.

\section*{Null Calibration and Known-Truth Simulations}

Calibration targeted the revised evidence profile, not the removed binary
retention rule. Expression was permuted 2,000 times in the frozen TCGA-LIHC
CDC20 OS population (365 participants; 130 events), preserving endpoint
records and a prespecified 1,091-day RMST horizon. Four known-truth scenarios
each used 2,000 datasets of \(n=300\): null; linear PH with HR 1.5 per SD;
no effect before time 2.5 followed by HR 2; and U-shaped log hazard
\(\log(1.8)(x^2-1)\). Every replicate refitted linear Cox, spline
nonlinearity, marker-term \texttt{cox.zph}, fixed-horizon RMST and four grouped
methods. Within-replicate Holm used the Lau94 p-value for maxstat. Wilson intervals
quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty.

In the observed and simulated null experiments, linear Cox, spline and marker
PH rejection remained near 5\%, and none of their Wilson intervals excluded
0.05. Grouped-family rejection was 3.2--3.5\%; both grouped Holm intervals and
both Lau94 intervals lay below 0.05, documenting conservative calibration
under the dependent cutpoints. Naive maxstat rejected 38.2--39.6\%, versus
1.8--2.4\% with Lau94. Under linear PH, power was 99.2\% for continuous Cox,
94.3\% for Lau94 maxstat, 93.9\% for median, 93.5\% for upper quartile,
97.8\% for outer quartiles and 96.4\% for the grouped Holm family. This
alternative supports the continuous-first default in this design but does not
claim universal power dominance; outer quartiles increase contrast while
discarding the middle half of participants. The PH diagnostic rejected 88.2\%
under the delayed effect, and the spline test rejected 100\% under the U-shaped
effect. Under observed permutation, median-split log-rank and Cox
\(-\log_{10}p\) values correlated 1.000; their correlations with RMST were
0.664 and 0.665. These are therefore related summaries, while PH modifies
interpretation. The benchmark includes replicate-level data, source hashes,
seeds, versions and the complete design.

\section*{Runtime and Concurrency Benchmark}

The public v1 asynchronous API was benchmarked from submission through the
first terminal job response on a Mac13,2 with Apple M1 Ultra, 20 logical CPUs
and 64\,GiB host memory. Docker Engine 29.4.1 exposed 20 CPUs and 7.75\,GiB to
the arm64 stack. A unique display-only title or session label forced each
uncached result without changing its statistical specification; this was not
an operating-system page-cache or fresh data-import benchmark. The exact
single-analysis request was then resubmitted to measure the completed-job
cache.

All jobs in the run shared one isolated client session and remained subject to
the public contract: two global workers, queue capacity 50, five active jobs
per client, and request limits of 25 batch analyses and 72 multiverse cells.
The service returns HTTP 429 with \texttt{Retry-After} when per-client or
hourly limits are reached and HTTP 503 with \texttt{Retry-After} when the queue
is full; stale jobs are requeued at most twice. These controls bound process
creation but the two-job probe is not a saturation load test.
The two-job probe observed both workers running and 2.53\,s of execution
overlap. Batch children execute serially within one worker slot. Queue and
compute intervals came from server timestamps; wall time was measured by the
client. Peak memory is the observed aggregate backend-plus-worker Docker
cgroup usage sampled approximately once per second, so sub-second allocator
peaks may be missed. The raw JSON, CSV and checksum manifest bind the workload
definitions, service limits, source commit, pipeline versions and data
snapshot.
