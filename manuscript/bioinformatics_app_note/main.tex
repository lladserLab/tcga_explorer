\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Summary:}
TCGA-TRACE is a TCGA transcriptomic-survival web application that makes
continuous Cox analysis primary, keeps grouped estimates as declared
sensitivities and exports the cohort, expression, specification family,
diagnostics and software as a reconstructable, server-signed record.
In 2,000 cohort-preserving permutations, continuous Cox rejected 5.0\% whereas
naive maxstat rejected 39.6\% versus 1.8\% with Lau94; continuous Cox had
99.2\% power under simulated linear proportional hazards, and six isolated
arm64/amd64 reruns reproduced three frozen analyses.
\textbf{Availability and Implementation:}
The registration-free service is available at
\url{https://apps.cienciavida.org/tcga_explorer/}. Source code and
reproducibility materials are MIT-licensed at
\url{https://github.com/lladserLab/tcga_explorer}; the exact submitted release,
benchmarks and 33-cohort snapshot manifest are archived at
\texttt{ARCHIVE-DOI-PENDING}. The Dockerized application uses FastAPI, React
and R.
\textbf{Contact:} email@example.org.
\textbf{Supplementary information:} Supplementary data are available with the
manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Transcriptomic survival associations can prioritize hypotheses, but their
apparent strength depends on endpoint, eligibility, RNA-sample selection,
expression scale, cutpoint and model. Outcome-optimized cutpoints are
particularly vulnerable when alternatives are hidden
\citep{altmanRoyston2006}.

Established resources cover complementary parts of this workflow. GEPIA2 and
KM Plotter support broad gene and signature screening
\citep{gepia22019,kmplotter2021}; cSurvival implements two-predictor cutoff
searches \citep{csurvival2022}; DoSurvive broadens multi-omic survival models
\citep{dousurvive2023}; and PESSA provides ssGSEA, continuous/grouped Cox and
Schoenfeld tests \citep{pessa2024}. These methods are prior art, and several are
broader or more specialized than TCGA-TRACE (Supplementary Table S1). The
narrower unresolved problem is exporting the exact endpoint, participants, RNA
samples, score construction, diagnostics, multiplicity scope and software as
one reconstructable run.

TCGA-TRACE was developed for researchers evaluating transcriptomic survival
hypotheses. It connects endpoint-aware cohorts to single-gene, signature,
interaction and pan-cancer analyses. Rather than proposing a new estimator, it
provides a verifiable run record and a declared multiverse/specification curve
\citep{steegen2016multiverse,simonsohn2020specification};
Supplementary Table S1 compares representative resources, and
Figure~\ref{fig:trace-workflow} summarizes the reporting workflow.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data and cohort construction}

TCGA-TRACE links retrospective bulk RNA-seq matrices from the Genomic Data
Commons \citep{gdcDataset2026} to TCGA-CDR overall survival (OS),
disease-specific survival (DSS), disease-free interval (DFI) and
progression-free interval (PFI) \citep{liu2018tcga}. Endpoints
require at least 10 linked participants and five events as operational QC.
After filters and endpoint completeness, the requested score is required before
a documented biospecimen rule selects one expression-complete RNA sample per
participant. Adjusted models repeat the 10-participant/five-event check after
complete-case filtering.
For DSS, DFI and PFI, TCGA-CDR status 0/1/2 denotes censoring, the endpoint
event and an endpoint-specific competing death. Kaplan--Meier/Cox censor code
2, whereas companion cumulative-incidence, Gray-test and Fine--Gray outputs
retain it; cause-specific HRs and subdistribution HRs are reported as distinct
estimands.

\subsection{Survival analyses}

Genes and mean, z-score or weighted signatures are supported (Supplementary
Table S2). Z-score signatures depend on the eligible run population and are not
transportable across endpoints or filters; weighted scores use
\(\sum_g w_g x_{ig}/\sum_g |w_g|\). Primary Cox effects are per score SD. A
three-parameter restricted cubic spline (knots 5, 35, 65 and 95) reports a
median-referenced profile and nonlinearity test when at least 30 events are
available \citep{harrell2015rms}.

Secondary Kaplan--Meier, grouped Cox and RMST use median, tertile, quartile,
custom-percentile or maxstat groups. Lau94 corrects the maxstat rank-test
\(p\), but grouped estimates remain post-selection. The frozen benchmark uses
four two-group rules (maxstat, median, upper and outer quartiles), Holm within
each scenario and separate BH families for continuous and nonlinear tests. Its
11 scenarios were registered after exploratory triage as software stress tests,
not prospective biomarker validation; OS is primary except UVM/BAP1 DSS, with
two linked endpoint sensitivities (Supplementary Methods).

All Cox models use Efron ties and may include an exact complete-case adjustment
for age, stage, grade, gender, race or uploaded covariates. Marker and global
\texttt{cox.zph} tests are cautions, not exclusion rules
\citep{grambschTherneau1994}. Marker \(p<0.05\) triggers fixed two-year primary
and one-/five-year sensitivity splits with clustered variance and a Wald
late/early ratio. Each period requires five events and the late period 10
entrants. RMST uses a group-independent horizon and support check. Every Cox
fit reports parameters and events per parameter; low-information or unstable
fits add, but do not replace the standard estimate with, a Firth sensitivity.
Crossed signatures add a continuous z-scored interaction.

Pan-cancer effects are pooled only after conversion to a common input-score
unit and only within one endpoint/model family, using REML, HKSJ inference and
a prediction interval. Within-cohort SD effects, z-score signatures, mixed
endpoints and availability-selected adjustments are not pooled.

\subsection{Web server and run record}

The React/FastAPI/R application defaults to one gene, OS, log2(TPM+1), no
filters, age adjustment and a median grouped sensitivity, with continuous Cox
shown first. Every completed run creates audit, reconstruction and signed
receipt downloads. A separate multiverse freezes up to 72
endpoint--score--cutpoint cells, retains failures and adjusts unique continuous
and grouped families separately. It is the prespecification mechanism; the
optional browser-local history is post hoc convenience only.

Operating characteristics used 2,000 expression permutations and 2,000
simulations per null, linear PH, delayed non-PH and U-shaped mechanism.
Wilson intervals quantify rejection uncertainty. Audit exports bind source
identifiers, expression components, participants, models, software and results;
a standalone R runner, lockfile and pinned container reconstruct two-group
runs. An Ed25519 receipt signs the exact audit bytes and run hash. Its archived
fingerprint and rotation policy support server-origin verification, not
scientific correctness, prespecification or append-only time.

\section{Case Studies and Evaluation}

\subsection*{Technical validation}

Three frozen exports---single-gene (365 participants), weighted-signature (79)
and crossed-signature (80)---passed reconstruction. Six isolated arm64/amd64
reruns matched core outputs (Supplementary Table S3), and Ed25519 rejected
altered audit bytes after internal hashes were recomputed. This addresses drift
and unverifiable origin, not fraud by a trusted signing service.

The registered panel retains supported, unsupported, nonlinear, sparse-event and
PH-discordant results (Supplementary Table S4): six linear and one nonlinear
effect had BH support; grouped Holm support was 4/4 in five scenarios, 3/4 in
one and 0/4 in five. Maxstat exceeded the median-split absolute log-HR in all
10 finite comparisons. Across scenarios, the median maxstat/median-split ratio
was 1.80 and the median maxstat/continuous-implied amplification was 1.88.
UVM/BAP1 was non-finite because all 21 events entered one maxstat group.
EMP3/LGG attenuated over time, whereas
CA9/KIRC was nonlinear (\(q_{\mathrm{NL}}=0.022\)): HRs relative to median were
1.89 at the 5th, 0.86 at the 75th and 1.02 at the 95th percentile, indicating
risk concentrated at low expression. A three-case cBioPortal check
\citep{cbioportal2012} retained full, direction-only and null agreement. The
broader and diagnostic workflows remain visible in Supplementary Tables
S5--S6. The four cases below were chosen post hoc for explanation, not
inference.

\subsection{Case 1: CDC20 in Hepatocellular Cancer}

A published CDC20--FCN3 model associated CDC20 with survival in TCGA and ICGC
hepatocellular carcinoma \citep{lai2022cdc20}. For CDC20 alone, 365 participants
and 130 events gave HR/SD 1.65 (1.38--1.98), age-adjusted HR 1.67
(1.39--2.01), 4/4 grouped support and median-split RMST difference -207 days
at \(\tau=1091\). This is directional, not independent, concordance.

\subsection{Case 2: BUB1B--PINK1 in ACC}

An independent Brazilian qRT-PCR cohort prioritized BUB1B with PINK1
\citep{fragoso2012bub1b}. Its TCGA RNA-seq analogue
\((\mathrm{BUB1B}-\mathrm{PINK1})/2\) gave HR/SD 2.85 (1.90--4.26) and
stage-adjusted HR 2.15 (1.42--3.26) in 79 participants/28 events. The median
split gave HR 4.12 (1.75--9.71) and RMST difference -460 days at
\(\tau=1826\). This cross-cohort/assay agreement corroborates direction, not
the original assay or threshold.

\subsection{Case 3: BIRC5 Across TCGA Cohorts}

A 33-cancer study linked higher BIRC5 to worse OS
\citep{faldtBeding2022birc5}. TCGA-TRACE found 18/32 effects at FDR \(<0.10\)
and common-scale REML HR 1.20 (HKSJ 1.06--1.37), but \(I^2=86.1\%\) and the
prediction interval was 0.64--2.24. Thus within-TCGA literature concordance
does not imply a universal pan-cancer effect.

\subsection{Case 4: BAP1/PRAME in Uveal Melanoma}

Published BAP1/PRAME immunohistochemistry stratified uveal-melanoma risk
\citep{han2023bap1prame}. TCGA RNA groups differed globally (\(p=0.002\)), but
the continuous interaction (\(p=0.637\)) and age-adjusted Firth sensitivity
(\(p=0.126\); 21 events) were unsupported. Separately, single-gene BAP1 DSS
gave median-split Gray \(p=0.00046\) and Fine--Gray HR 0.17 (0.06--0.52) with
two competing deaths. This supports a single-gene direction, not an RNA
interaction or reproduction of the protein assay. This was the deliberately
non-confirmatory case.

\subsection{Calibration and Runtime}

Under observed-cohort expression permutation, rejection proportions were
5.0\% for Cox, 5.8\% for nonlinearity and 5.1\% for marker PH; grouped Holm
was conservative at 3.2\%. Naive maxstat rejected 39.6\% versus 1.8\% with
Lau94. Under linear PH, power was 99.2\% for continuous Cox, 94.3\% for
Lau94, 93.9\% for median, 93.5\% for upper quartile, 97.8\% for outer
quartiles and 96.4\% for grouped Holm. Marker PH detected 88.2\% of delayed
effects and the spline 100\% of U-shaped effects (Supplementary Table S7).

On an Apple M1 Ultra Docker host, wall times were 5.12\,s for one uncached run,
32.5\,s for a 10-run batch, 219\,s for a 72-cell multiverse, 7.18\,s for 33
pan-cancer cohorts and 0.037\,s cached. The bounded public queue has two workers
and returns explicit retry instructions at limits (Supplementary Table S8).

\section{Future Plans}

TCGA-TRACE currently supports retrospective bulk-TCGA screening rather than
independent biomarker validation. Its literature-anchored cases test the
reporting contract; they do not reproduce protein, mutation or qRT-PCR assays.
Z-score and weighted signatures are transparent summaries rather than
replacements for pathway-activity methods such as ssGSEA. Generic clinical
fields and user-provided covariates cannot remove residual confounding by
molecular subtype, tumour purity or unmeasured factors, and a common expression
unit does not erase pan-cancer heterogeneity.

Future releases will prioritize independent-cohort evaluation and
cohort-specific covariate modules. An externally witnessed timestamp or
transparency log could add append-only publication time to the current signed
receipt, while low-event simulations across several cohort structures and
selection-aware maxstat intervals will extend calibration. Cross-architecture
reruns used locked versions and do not establish numerical stability after
future R/package upgrades. TCGA-TRACE keeps declared choices, effects,
diagnostics and provenance beside supported and unsupported results;
biological and clinical claims still require independent data.
